OLED Care Privacy Policy

OLED Care, by ByteAcre · Effective 12 November 2026 (version 1.0)

OLED Care is a Windows app that reduces how long static images stay on your screens: it blacks out screens you aren't using and dims the taskbar while you don't use it. This policy explains what information the app uses and what it does with it. In short: OLED Care never takes screenshots or reads what is on your screen, never records keys or audio, and does not collect, send or share any of your data. Everything stays on your PC.

Information the app uses (on your PC only, never stored or sent)

  • Your screens. Their names (for example "LG ULTRAGEAR" or "Built-in display"), position and resolution, so you can choose which screens to protect and the app can cover them exactly.
  • Whether you are using the PC. The time since your last mouse, keyboard, touch or game controller input and where the mouse pointer is (for game controllers, only that a button was pressed), to know when a screen is idle and to bring it back the moment you return.
  • Which apps are open where. Which app is in front, which app names have windows on which screen, and whether a window fills a whole screen (full-screen games, videos and presentations), so the app never blacks out a screen you are watching. It looks only at window positions and app names, never at their contents or titles.
  • Whether something is playing sound. Windows' own volume level meters tell the app which apps are making sound (a level between 0 and 1, the same number the Windows volume mixer shows), so it doesn't black out while you watch a video or listen to music. No audio is ever recorded, listened to or analysed.
  • Whether a microphone is in use. So that your screen never goes black during a call or a meeting, the app checks which apps are using a microphone right now, from Windows' own audio sessions and from the record Windows keeps for its microphone privacy icon. OLED Care never opens, records or listens to the microphone.
  • The key or click that wakes a black screen. Only while a screen is blacked out, the app watches the keyboard and mouse so that the first key or click brings the picture back without also doing something in the app you couldn't see. Nothing is recorded; this stops as soon as the screen is back.
  • The taskbar. Where it is (to dim it), and, only if you choose "Hide the taskbar automatically", Windows' auto-hide setting, which the app saves first and puts back when you pause or exit it.

Free trial and purchase

OLED Care is a paid app with a free trial, sold in the Microsoft Store. Purchases are made in Microsoft's own purchase window; ByteAcre never sees your payment details. To know whether you are in the trial or have bought the app, OLED Care asks the Microsoft Store on your PC for its license. That check is handled by Microsoft under the Microsoft Privacy Statement; OLED Care does not send anything about you with it.

Information stored on your PC

OLED Care saves the following only on your PC, in the app's own data folder:

  • your settings: which screens are OLED, the idle and second-screen times, the taskbar option, the app names on your exception list, and your original taskbar auto-hide setting while the app has changed it;
  • the last known license state (trial or full), so the app works offline;
  • a small log file of what the app did (for example "Black-out: Built-in display (Idle)"), which helps with troubleshooting. The log never leaves your PC unless you choose to send it to us.

Uninstalling the app removes its data folder.

What the app does not do

  • It does not take screenshots or read what is shown on your screen.
  • It does not record what you type or click.
  • It does not record, play or listen to audio, and it never uses your microphone.
  • It does not send any information to ByteAcre or anyone else. Its only online activity is the Microsoft Store license and purchase check described above.
  • It does not include advertising, analytics or tracking.
  • It does not access your files, contacts, location, camera or microphone.

Start with Windows

OLED Care starts when you sign in to Windows, so your screens are always cared for. It turns this on at its first start and tells you so. You can turn it off in the app, in its tray menu, or in Windows Settings > Apps > Startup.

Children

OLED Care does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.
